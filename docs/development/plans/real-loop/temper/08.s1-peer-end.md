# temper-08.s1 A peer's end ends its call

Added by ruling 123, from the designer's answer to 08.3's question, for
session 08 (`08-real-loop.md`): it runs before 08.3, which waits for it.

Scope: temper-service takes a peer's end, during a call it handed jig's
engine end, as the call's end (`client/README.md`, 8; jig's
`protocol/client.md`, 17.4), so that a call's stream is free once its
process exits (tools.md, 8.2).
Needs: 08.2; the same session, before 08.3.
Files: `crates/temper-service/src/server.rs`; one test file of
`crates/temper-service/src/tests/`.
Shape: in `server.rs`'s `io::Event::Stream` arm, an `Up::End`
(`skein_lib::stream::Up`) on a connection whose `call` is `Some` goes to
`http::up` as now, then `end(connection, false, out)`, the
`io::Event::Failed` arm's abort. The `io::Event::Closed` arm then sends
`Routed::Http { call, event: http::Event::Closed }`, from which jig's
mount sends `StreamGone`. No skein or jig code, and no pin.
Test shape: `the_next_clients_stream_opens_once_a_peer_ends_its_own`,
under 20 ms: on the small profile's two streams, three clients one
after another each open the stream, read the hello and end their side;
it asserts only that the third's stream opens with no virtual time past
a heartbeat.
Proof: unit, its test; then 08.3's story as written, aiming at 1 s.
Don't: raise `CALLS_AT_ONCE`, the profile's streams or the heartbeat;
wait or retry in the caller; change skein or jig (ruling 118).
Size: about 15 production lines.
Removes: nothing.
