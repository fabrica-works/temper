# temper's store

Provisional, 2026-10-05, revised 2026-10-14. What temper keeps in jig's
store, and how: its families, the forge's records, under the
application's tag; their keys, their records and temper's store
version; and the restart's ranges. The store itself is jig's:
`jig-protocol-store`, skein's key-value store in the engine's process,
with transcripts as files and secrets in a second store, whose design
this document cites as jig's `protocol/store.md`. temper's part is the
crate `temper-protocol-store` (protocol.md, section 5), engine only.
What is open is listed in section 7.

## 1. In one page

- **jig's store, temper's families.** The engine's protocol layer owns
  jig's store component; temper adds its own records beside jig's,
  under the application's tag, in the same commits (jig's
  `protocol/store.md`, section 12).
- **One family per forge record.** Each variant of the forge connector's
  `Stored` is a family, a keyed adoption's outcome among them. Nothing
  else of temper's is stored: temper has no index of its own and no
  secret record; the core's records, people's and tasks' are jig's.
- **Keys sort as the forge's keys do:** a family byte in the variants'
  order, then the key's fields by jig's key rule; a property test holds
  each encoder to the derived order (section 3).
- **Records from a schema,** generated by skein's generator, translated
  to and from the domain's by total functions, with golden bytes and
  fuzzing; `STORE_VERSION` names their encodings (section 4).
- **All live.** The forge's restart step restores every family of
  temper's as one interval, the root's `Range::Forge`; nothing of
  temper's is loaded on demand (section 5).
- **The core's restart ranges are jig's:** the root's core ranges are
  asked as jig's coarser ones and filtered, and its census takes jig's
  index walk as it comes, an ended task per index row, its cursor the
  index row's key (section 5).
- **Sized from limits.** The largest key and record follow from the
  forge's limits, and jig's component checks them with its own at start
  (section 4).
- **The real store in temper's worlds,** over skein-kv's in-memory mode,
  and on the simulator's crashing disk in the one-process world
  (section 6).
- **jig's, cited, not restated:**
  - transcripts: one file per task, written before their commit (jig's
    `protocol/store.md`, section 8);
  - secrets: a second store, which holds no record of temper's in
    standalone (jig's `protocol/store.md`, section 9; credentials.md,
    section 6);
  - opening: the format row and the header checked before the root
    starts (jig's `protocol/store.md`, section 10);
  - fill: reported with each commit, new work refused above the
    high-water mark (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 11.2);
  - retention: the core's ended work shrunk past horizons (jig's
    `protocol/store.md`, 11.1); temper's families have none (section 7).

## 2. temper's families

One per variant of `temper_engine_domain_forge::Stored`
(`crates/temper-engine-domain-forge/src/boundary.rs`), in the order of
its variants, which is also the order of its `Key`. Each is restored
whole at the forge's restart step (section 5), and bounded by the
forge's limits (`temper_engine_domain_forge::Limits`, configuration.md,
section 7).

| Byte | Family | Key | What a row holds | Bounded by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `Call` | `Call(NamedCall)`: task, attempt, completion, position | the connector's part of a live named call | `named_calls` |
| 1 | `ProposedEffect` | `ProposedEffect(u64)`: the proposal's number | an effect awaiting an authority holder's decision | `tasks` |
| 2 | `Repository` | `Repository(Repository)`: forge, repository | an adopted repository: its project, role, kinds, protection and settings | `repositories` |
| 3 | `Hold` | `Hold(Name)` | a held resource, its task, its writer and its drift | `holds` |
| 4 | `Names` | `Names(u64)`: the task | the live resources one task names | `tasks` |
| 5 | `Subscription` | `Subscription { task, topic }` | one task's subscription to a topic | `subscriptions` |
| 6 | `BranchHead` | `BranchHead(Name)` | a branch's last observed tip | the client's `resources` |
| 7 | `PullState` | `PullState(Name)` | a pull request's last published state | the client's `resources` |
| 8 | `Ci` | `Ci { repository, head }` | the last CI verdict published for a commit | `subscriptions` |
| 9 | `Landed` | `Landed(Commit)` | a merge temper made, and its task | `landings` |
| 10 | `Entry` | `Entry(u64)`: the entry's number | an unsettled outbox entry, held or not | `entries` |
| 11 | `Client` | `Client(client::Key)`: `Live(Resource)` or `Repository(Repository)` | the client's working set: a live resource's row, or a repository's listing mark | the client's `resources` and `repositories` |
| 12 | `Change` | `Change(u64)`: the task | a change procedure's state | `changes` |
| 13 | `Issue` | `Issue(u64)`: the goal | a goal's issue projection | `issues` |
| 14 | `Release` | `Release(u64)`: the task | a closing task's release | `tasks` |
| 15 | `Application` | `Application(u64)`: the people child's token for the request | a keyed adoption's outcome, kept until its retention ends (jig's `domain/connectors.md`, section 5: a connector keeps its keyed requests' outcomes durably) | `applications` |

- **Loaded:** every family at the forge's restart step, and never
  otherwise. No party reads them: the engine serves none of the forge's
  client parts but the adoption form (client.md, section 4).
- **Written** in the root's decisions, as the forge asks: its `Save` and
  `Erase` become the root's `Write::Save(Record::Forge { row })` and
  `Write::Erase(Key::Forge(key))`, which the routing encodes into jig's
  application writes, `Put { key, value }` and `Remove { key }`, in the
  same commit as jig's (jig's `protocol/store.md`, sections 3 and 6).
- **A new variant** of `Stored` is a new family at the next byte, in a
  plan that moves `STORE_VERSION` (section 4).

## 3. Keys

- **Under the application's tag,** 2, which jig's component prefixes and
  never lets temper's bytes leave (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 4.2).
  temper's own bytes start with the family's byte (section 2), then the
  key's fields.
- **By jig's rule** (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 4.1), written with
  skein-kv's key writer: a variant's index as a tag, then its fields in
  order; every number big-endian as eight bytes, narrower ones widened;
  a byte string escaped and terminated; a commit as its 32 bytes, a
  byte string; a list of byte strings (a branch's segments) as each
  string marked, then an end mark. So:
  - `Name` is the forge, the repository, then its `What`: 0 for the
    repository, 1 and the branch's segments, 2 and a pull request's
    number, 3 and an issue's;
  - `Topic` is its variant (0 `Landings`, 1 `Ci`, 2 `Pull`, 3
    `Participation`), then the repository, then the branch, the commit
    or the number;
  - the client's key is its variant (0 `Live`, 1 `Repository`), then its
    resource or repository, a resource's `What` tagged as `Name`'s is,
    its branch one byte string.
- **Written out, not generated:** each family has a small encoder and
  decoder in `temper-protocol-store`. A property test holds each encoder
  to the order `Key` derives, on generated keys of every family, and
  each decoder to its encoder; a decoder refuses a tag it does not know,
  a number past its field's width, and trailing bytes.
- **A key and its record agree.** A row's value holds the whole record,
  its key's fields among them; the routing checks that a decoded
  record's key encodes to the row's key, and a row where they differ is
  one that does not decode, which stops the engine (jig's
  `protocol/store.md`, 7.1).

## 4. Records and the store version

- **One schema,** in `temper-protocol-store`, with a record for each
  family and for every value those records carry (the client's entries,
  effects, writes, live rows and cached answers; the change's and the
  issues' states; a named call's payload, with the forge's answers and
  errors), generated by skein's generator (skein's `codec.md`). The
  domain's values are translated to and from the generated ones by small
  total functions, exhaustive both ways, so a domain change to a durable
  field breaks a translation's build.
- **Collections** the domain bounds by their bytes in all are one piece,
  and those it bounds by a count of its own are counted lists (jig's
  `protocol/store.md`, 5.1). The schema's limits are made from the
  forge's limits, and checked against the schema's ceilings at start.
- **Each record carries its family's version,** as every generated
  record does (skein's `codec.md`, section 5).
- **`STORE_VERSION`,** temper's store version: one number for all of
  temper's families, 1 at first, moved once per plan, at the first
  landing that changes an encoding of temper's in the store
  (protocol.md, section 9). The routing gives it to jig's component,
  whose format row records it; a store written with another refuses to
  open, saying so, and nothing is translated before upgrades are
  designed (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 5.2 and 5.3).
- **Sizes.** `temper-protocol-store` computes, from the forge's limits,
  the largest key and the largest value of its families, and hands them
  with `STORE_VERSION` to jig's component, which checks them with its own
  against skein-kv's limits, and the start is refused when they do not
  fit (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 5.4).
- **Golden bytes** for each family at the current version, a fuzz
  target for each family's decoder, and bounds at their limit and one
  past.

## 5. The restart

- **One step, one interval.** The core's restart script asks for the
  forge connector's step (`RestoreConnector`, jig's `domain/engine.md`,
  section 6). The root then loads `Range::Forge` a page at a time,
  restores each row into the forge (its `Event::Restore`), and ends the
  step with its `Restored`.
- **The routing maps `Range::Forge`** to one interval: every key under
  temper's tag. It asks jig's component to restore it as the
  application's interval, and jig's component serves its pages as bytes
  (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 7.2 and 12). The routing encodes the
  root's cursor with temper's key encoder, decodes each row with
  temper's codecs before the root sees it, and keeps the longest prefix
  of the page within the root's rows and decoded bytes, its cursor the
  last key kept.
- **Family by family,** in the variants' order, since the family's byte
  leads each key. The forge restores each row into its own table and
  reconciles them all at `Restored`, so it takes the rows in any order.
- **Every row the predicate admits.** `Range::contains` admits every
  forge key and no core key; a test checks the interval against it on
  generated keys of both (section 6).
- **Nothing else is read:** no load, get or party's read of temper's
  families follows the restart.
- **The core's ranges are jig's, filtered.** jig's restart ranges
  (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 4.3 and 7.2) are coarser than the root's
  core ranges, so the routing asks for the jig ranges that hold a root
  range and keeps each row the root's range admits: `CoreRange::Tasks`,
  the tasks child's live families with the goals' projections, which
  are core rows, is jig's `[Core, Tasks]`, in that order, which is also
  the keys' order; `CoreRange::{Calls, RunProofs}` are jig's `[Core]`.
  A root cursor is a key of the root's range, and names the jig
  interval that holds it. A page whose rows the filter all drops, while
  jig's walk goes on, is asked again from jig's cursor before the root
  hears of it, so a page that goes on keeps a row, and its cursor is the
  last key kept.
- **The census is jig's index walk.** `Range::Census` is jig's census
  (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 7.2 and 7.4): its index rows walked in
  order (the conversations' index, `ConversationOldest`, then each
  party's unread results, `Result`, parties in the census's order, each
  interval by key), and each joined to the ended task it names. So a
  page's rows are ended tasks in the walk's order, the same task once
  for each index row that names it, and its cursor is the key of the
  last index row walked, not a row's key. The root checks that each row
  is an ended task and that the cursor is an index key the census
  admits, past the page's start in the walk's order; a page may hold no
  row and go on. It neither orders nor dedupes the rows: jig's core
  restores them as they come, keeping per party how far it has read, as
  jig's reference root hands them. A cut census page stops the restart,
  as every cut restart page does.

## 6. Testing

Within the suites' budgets (testing.md, section 2):

- **Step tests** in `temper-protocol-store`: each family's key encoder
  against the derived order, and its decoder against its encoder; the
  interval of `Range::Forge` against `Range::contains` over generated
  keys; each codec both ways, with golden bytes; each translation both
  ways, a domain value equal to itself after a round trip; the schema's
  bounds at their limit and one past; the sizes against the forge's
  limits.
- **Fuzz targets** for each family's decoder and for the key decoder.
- **The real store in the domain worlds,** as jig's `protocol/store.md`,
  section 14 has it: temper's engine worlds run jig's component over
  skein-kv's in-memory mode, with temper's families encoded by
  `temper-protocol-store`, in place of jig's fake store (skein's
  `testing-strategy.md`, section 4: a fake of the service's own
  component retires once the real one exists). So every story runs the
  real keys, codecs and interval. Faults come from the world: it holds
  commits between the root and the store, delays them, fails one to stop
  the engine, and a crash keeps what reached the store.
- **The one-process world** (`tests/one-process`, testing.md, 2.5): the
  store on skein's crashing disk, the process killed and restarted at
  drawn moments; after each restart the forge's records are those of
  the last acknowledged commit, or of that and one whole uncertain one,
  never part of one.
- **The store's own world** is jig's (jig's `protocol/store.md`, section
  14); temper adds none.

## 7. Open questions

- **`Landed` grows with history.** A merge temper made is kept for
  own-change news, and no row is erased; once the forge's `landings`
  limit is reached, later merges are not recorded, and news of them reads
  as another's landing. Default: bounded by the forge's limits, no
  retention. A row could go once no live goal's subscription could hear
  of its merge.
- **Upgrades:** reading an older `STORE_VERSION` as it loads, or a pass
  at opening that rewrites it, once a deployment's data must outlive a
  release. Default: jig's (jig's `protocol/store.md`, section 16), a store
  of another version refused.
- **Indexes for the forge's client parts,** once panels are served
  (client.md, section 4): a family of small rows per view, as jig's
  indexes are (jig's `protocol/store.md`, 7.4). Default: none until
  they are served.
